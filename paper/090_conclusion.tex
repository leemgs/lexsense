% -------------------- English Version --------------------

This study takes a step toward \textit{inclusive access} to legal and government information by introducing \textbf{LexSense}, a multilingual AI framework that integrates change detection, multilingual natural language processing, concept-drift monitoring, evidence-grounded analysis, and LLM-based reporting. Evaluated on English, Korean, and French, LexSense demonstrated high accuracy and significant efficiency gains (70\% faster summarization) while keeping latency under 3.5s, and stress tests on Spanish, Arabic, and Vietnamese showed its potential to extend to low-resource languages. The release of GovSense-1k, open-source code, and Dockerized environments further enhances reproducibility and practical applicability.

Beyond technical achievements, this work contributes a conceptual foundation that bridges natural language processing, legal and policy informatics, and responsible AI. By embedding factuality auditing, transparency, and human-oversight mechanisms, LexSense advances not only system efficiency but also responsible deployment for non-experts and multilingual communities.

Looking forward, we envision extending this framework to additional low-resource jurisdictions, strengthening long-term robustness through automated drift detection, and improving plain-language summarization for non-experts. In doing so, LexSense can evolve from a practical prototype into a cornerstone infrastructure for \textit{inclusive access} to public legal and governmental knowledge.
